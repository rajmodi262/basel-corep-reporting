## 1. Request

1. **Regulatory:** the designated authority raised the countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB) rate
   applicable to the bank's exposures from **0.75% to 1.00%** (CRD Art 130, 136, 140).
2. **Data:** the collateral management system delivered property valuations for the **6 residential
   mortgages** flagged by DQ-03 as having no valuation.

## 2. Regulatory analysis

**CCyB.** The CCyB is part of the *combined buffer requirement* (CRD Art 128(6)), which sits on top of
TSCR to form the **overall capital requirement** (OCR). It changes *requirements*, not *capital* or
*risk-weighted assets*. So:

- C 03.00 OCR rows r0160-r0210 rise by 25 bp;
- the CET1 surplus after buffers (r0220) falls by 0.25% × TREA;
- own funds (C 01.00), risk exposure amounts (C 02.00) and the leverage ratio (C 47.00) do **not** move.
  Buffers do not apply to the leverage ratio except for G-SIIs (CRR Art 92(1a)).

**Valuations.** A residential mortgage gets loan-splitting (20% up to 55% of the property value,
counterparty weight on the remainder, CRR Art 125) only if the property is valued and the valuation
is monitored, at least every three years for residential property (CRR Art 208(3)(a)). So:

- a valuation dated after 2022-12-31 is usable, and the mortgage moves from **Retail (r0140)** to
  **Secured by residential property (r0150, split into r0151 / r0152)**;
- an older valuation is **not** usable. It is reported (new rule DQ-09), and the mortgage stays retail.
- Moving exposure from 75% to partly 20% lowers RWEA, so the SA subtotals and TREA (r0060, r0050,
  r0040, r0010, in both the TREA and S-TREA columns) fall, and every ratio and surplus in C 03.00
  that divides by or multiplies TREA moves.
